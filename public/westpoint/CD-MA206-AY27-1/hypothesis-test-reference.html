Hypothesis Tests — Course Reference

Every mean/proportion test we teach, and which are assessed on the TEE

This reference covers the inference procedures for means and proportions taught across Lessons 22–26. Regression t-tests and the ANOVA F-test are separate families (Lessons 29+) and are not included here.

The six test procedures

# Parameter Samples Structure Test Distribution (df) Key detail
1 Mean \(\mu\) one — one-sample \(t\) \(t\), \(df = n-1\) \(\sigma\) unknown \(\Rightarrow\) use \(s\); large \(n \Rightarrow t \approx z\)
2 \(\mu_1 - \mu_2\) two independent, equal variance two-sample \(t\) (pooled) \(t\), \(df = n_1 + n_2 - 2\) pooled SD
3 \(\mu_1 - \mu_2\) two independent, unequal variance two-sample \(t\) (Welch) \(t\), Welch–Satterthwaite \(df\) unpooled SE
4 \(\mu_d\) two paired / matched paired \(t\) (one-sample \(t\) on the differences) \(t\), \(df = n-1\) analyze the differences
5 Proportion \(p\) one — one-proportion \(z\) \(z\) conditions \(np_0,\ n(1-p_0) \ge 10\)
6 \(p_1 - p_2\) two independent two-proportion \(z\) \(z\) pooled SE for the test, unpooled SE for the CI

The combo grid: \(\{\text{one-sample},\ \text{two-sample}\} \times \{\text{mean},\ \text{proportion}\}\), with means splitting into paired vs. unpaired, and unpaired means splitting into pooled vs. Welch — six distinct procedures.

Small vs. large sample

“Small vs. large sample” is not a different test — for means it changes the condition check, not the procedure:

  • \(n > 20\) → CLT covers you; approximate normality is assumed.
  • \(n \le 20\) → the data (or the paired differences) must be roughly symmetric, with no strong skew or outliers.
  • Proportions are always large-sample \(z\), justified by the success–failure condition (not by \(n > 20\)).

TEE coverage

Test On the TEE? Where
One-sample \(t\) ✅ Dedicated part Part IV (caloric intake, \(H_a: \mu > 3200\))
Two-sample \(t\) ✅ Dedicated part Part V (VR vs. traditional, \(H_a: \mu_1 \ne \mu_2\), Welch \(df = 51.7\))
One-proportion \(z\) ✅ Dedicated part Part V (rifle qualification, \(H_a: p < 0.75\))
Paired \(t\) ⚠️ Concept only Part I MC (recognize the matched-pairs design)
Two-proportion \(z\) ❌ Not assessed —
Two-sample \(t\) (pooled vs. Welch) ◑ Welch only Part V uses Welch \(df\); the pooled variant is not separately assessed

Summary. Of the six procedures, three have a dedicated free-response part (one-sample \(t\), two-sample \(t\), one-proportion \(z\)). Paired \(t\) appears only as a multiple-choice concept check, and two-proportion \(z\) is taught but not assessed on the exam at all.

Across the assessed tests, the alternative-hypothesis directions are also varied: Part IV is one-sided \(>\), Part V two-sample is two-sided \(\ne\), and Part V proportion is one-sided \(<\) — one of each direction.
